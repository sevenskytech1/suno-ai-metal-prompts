# Quick Start: How to Use Suno AI Metal Prompts

Getting a good metal track out of Suno comes down to a simple process.

1. **Paste the prompt into the Style box.** Not the lyrics box. The style prompt controls the sound of the instruments and vocals.
2. **Write your lyrics separately** in the lyrics box, using section tags like `[Verse]` and `[Chorus]` (covered later in this guide).
3. **Generate a few versions.** Suno gives you multiple takes per prompt. Metal prompts especially benefit from this since drum and guitar tone can shift between generations.
4. **Change one thing at a time.** If a song comes out too soft, don't rewrite the whole prompt. Change just the vocal style or just the guitar tone and try again. This is the fastest way to learn what each word actually does.

**Beginner example:**

```
Modern death metal, low-tuned guitars, blast beats, guttural vocals, dark heavy production
```

That one line already beats "metal" or "heavy metal" by a wide margin, because it tells Suno the subgenre, the guitar tuning, the drum style, and the vocal style.

# How Suno Understands Metal Prompts

Suno reads your style prompt as a set of instructions, not a mood board. Every word you add narrows down what it generates. Think of the prompt as flowing through layers, in this order:

```
Genre
   ↓
Subgenre
   ↓
Tempo
   ↓
Guitars
   ↓
Drums
   ↓
Vocals
   ↓
Production
   ↓
Mood
```

**Genre** tells Suno the broad category (metal).
**Subgenre** narrows it down (death metal, doom metal, black metal, and so on). This is the single most important word in your whole prompt.
**Tempo** sets the speed. A number in BPM works better than words like "fast" or "slow."
**Guitars** describes tone and technique: low-tuned, distorted, palm-muted, tremolo-picked.
**Drums** describes the pattern: blast beats, double kick, half-time, mechanical.
**Vocals** describes delivery: guttural, screamed, clean, harsh growls.
**Production** describes the mix: raw, polished, dark, cinematic, lo-fi.
**Mood** ties it together: aggressive, cold, epic, crushing.

**Weak prompt:**

```
Heavy metal
```

This gives Suno almost nothing to work with. It will guess, and the guess is usually generic.

**Strong prompt:**

```
Modern death metal, 200 BPM, low-tuned guitars, blast beats, guttural vocals, dark aggressive production
```

Same genre, completely different result. The strong version tells Suno the subgenre, the exact tempo, the guitar tuning, the drum pattern, the vocal style, and the production feel. Every one of those details shows up in the final track.

# The Metal Prompt Formula

Once you understand the layers above, you can build any metal prompt using this template:

```
[Metal Genre], [Subgenre], [BPM], [Guitar Tone], [Drum Style], [Vocal Style], [Atmosphere], [Production]
```

Here's what each slot does and how to fill it:

| Slot | What it controls | Example values |
|---|---|---|
| Metal Genre | Anchors the base sound | metal, heavy metal |
| Subgenre | Defines the style rules | thrash, death, doom, black, metalcore, deathcore, industrial, power, symphonic, progressive |
| BPM | Sets the speed | 90 BPM, 140 BPM, 180 BPM, 220 BPM |
| Guitar Tone | Shapes the riffs | low-tuned, drop C, fuzz-heavy, tremolo-picked, palm-muted |
| Drum Style | Shapes the rhythm section | blast beats, double kick, half-time breakdown, mechanical drums |
| Vocal Style | Shapes the voice | guttural vocals, harsh growls, screamed vocals, clean vocals, choir vocals |
| Atmosphere | Sets the emotional tone | cold, epic, crushing, cinematic, raw |
| Production | Shapes the mix | dark, polished, lo-fi, orchestral, industrial |

**Example built from the formula:**

```
Metal, symphonic subgenre, 130 BPM, layered orchestral guitars, double kick drums, clean female vocals with choir backing, epic atmosphere, cinematic production
```

You don't need to fill every slot every time, but the more slots you fill, the more control you have.

# Metal Vocal Prompt Guide

Vocals make or break a metal prompt. Suno responds well to specific vocal terms, so pick the one that matches what you actually want to hear.

## Guttural vocals

Deep, low, throat-driven growls. Common in death metal and deathcore.

```
Death metal, low-tuned guitars, blast beats, deep guttural vocals, dark crushing production
```

## Harsh growls

Slightly higher and rougher than guttural vocals. Sits well in blackened or melodic death metal.

```
Melodic death metal, tremolo guitar leads, double kick drums, harsh growled vocals, atmospheric production
```

## Screamed vocals

Higher pitched, aggressive, common in metalcore and deathcore.

```
Metalcore, drop C guitars, breakdown riffs, aggressive screamed vocals, modern polished production
```

## Clean vocals

Melodic, sung vocals. Common in power metal, symphonic metal, and prog metal.

```
Power metal, soaring guitar leads, fast double kick, powerful clean vocals, epic production
```

## Mixed vocals

A blend of two styles in one song, often used to build contrast between verse and chorus.

```
Metalcore, drop-tuned riffs, half-time breakdown, screamed verses with clean sung chorus, dynamic production
```

> Tip: If Suno gives you vocals that sound too polite, add "harsh" or "aggressive" right next to the vocal type. Small adjectives change the delivery a surprising amount.

# Suno Lyrics Structure for Metal Songs

Suno reads structure tags in the lyrics box to decide how to arrange the song. For metal, these tags help keep the structure recognizable:

```
[Intro]
[Verse]
[Chorus]
[Breakdown]
[Guitar Solo]
[Outro]
```

A basic metalcore lyric layout might look like this:

```
[Intro]
(instrumental build-up)

[Verse]
Your verse lyrics here

[Chorus]
Your chorus lyrics here

[Breakdown]
(short, heavy, repeated lines work best here)

[Guitar Solo]

[Outro]
```

The `[Breakdown]` tag is especially useful in metalcore and deathcore. It tells Suno to drop the tempo feel and lean into a heavier, slower section, which pairs well with a style prompt that already includes "breakdown" or "half-time drums." Keep breakdown lyrics short. Repeated short phrases work better than long sentences, since the section is built around groove and impact, not storytelling.

# Common Suno Metal Problems and Fixes

| Problem | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Song sounds like pop rock | Prompt is too generic or missing vocal/guitar detail | Add "aggressive screamed vocals," "low-tuned guitars," "heavy distortion" |
| Guitars are weak | No tuning or technique specified | Add "drop tuning," "palm-muted riffs" |
| No breakdown | Drum and structure cues are missing | Add "metalcore breakdown," "half-time drums," and use the `[Breakdown]` lyric tag |
| Vocals are too clean | Vocal style wasn't specified clearly | Specify "guttural vocals" or "harsh growls" directly |
| Song feels too polished | Production term not specified | Add "raw production" or "lo-fi production" |
| Drums feel too simple | Drum style not named | Add "blast beats" or "double kick drums" specifically |

# Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the best Suno AI metal prompts?**
The best prompts name a specific subgenre, an exact BPM, a guitar tuning or technique, a drum pattern, and a vocal style. General prompts like "heavy metal" tend to produce generic results.

**Can Suno generate death metal?**
Yes. Naming "death metal" along with details like low-tuned guitars, blast beats, and guttural vocals gives Suno enough to work with to produce a convincing death metal track.

**How do I make Suno vocals heavier?**
Specify the exact vocal style you want, such as "guttural vocals," "harsh growls," or "screamed vocals," instead of leaving vocals unspecified or just writing "aggressive vocals."

**How do I create metalcore breakdowns?**
Add "breakdown" and "half-time drums" to your style prompt, and use a `[Breakdown]` tag in your lyrics with short, repeated lines.

**What BPM works best for metal?**
It depends on the subgenre. Doom metal often sits between 40-85 BPM. Death metal and black metal often run 160-220 BPM. Power metal typically sits between 140-190 BPM. Metalcore usually runs 130-165 BPM.

**Why does Suno make metal songs sound soft?**
Usually because the prompt is missing guitar tuning, drum pattern, or vocal style details. Adding those three elements is the fastest fix.

**How do I create industrial metal?**
Combine metal guitar and drum terms with electronic terms, such as "mechanical drums," "dark synth layers," and "electronic production," alongside your subgenre and vocal style.

**Can Suno create black metal?**
Yes. Prompts that include "tremolo-picked guitars," "blast beats," "raspy shrieking vocals," and "raw lo-fi production" tend to produce results that match the genre's raw, cold sound.

# Final Words

Good Suno metal prompts are specific, not clever. Naming the subgenre, tempo, guitar tuning, drum pattern, vocal style, and production tone gives you far more control than any single "magic word."

Metal depends on getting all of these working together: sound, rhythm, vocals, and production. Leave one out and the track drifts toward generic rock or pop.

Use the 100 prompts above as starting points, not final answers. Change one detail at a time, listen to what shifts, and build your own library of prompts that match the exact sound you're going for.
